CNA Apps · typeface shortlist

Five fonts, shown doing the actual job

Every sample below is a real screen from your apps — the month total, a claims list with plates, an OT row with clock times. Judge them on the numbers, because that is what your drivers stare at.

01

Inter Tight

My pick

Why: built for screens, and the tight version saves a surprising amount of width — useful when a plate, a date and a dollar figure share one phone row. Numbers are even and unfussy. It is the closest to what your apps already look like, so nothing will feel wrong on day one. Watch: it is a common choice, so it reads as clean rather than characterful.

02

IBM Plex Sans

Most characterful

Why: has an engineer's personality without being hard to read — slightly squared shapes that suit timesheets and dockets. Its matching monospace is excellent, so clock times and reference numbers can share a family. Watch: a little wider than Inter Tight, so crowded rows get tighter on a small phone.

03

Manrope

Friendliest

Why: rounder and warmer, and its heavy weight makes big totals look confident. Good if you want the apps to feel like a product rather than a form. Watch: the digits are a touch quirky at small sizes, and a dense claims table is where that shows most.

04

Public Sans

Plainest

Why: drawn for government forms, which is almost exactly your problem — dense, official, must not be misread. Nothing decorative gets in the way of a number. Watch: deliberately neutral, so it will never make the app look designed.

One thing before you choose

Your apps currently use whatever font the phone already has — Apple's on an iPhone, Google's on an Android. That costs nothing to load and never fails. Picking one of these means every phone downloads it, which is a small delay the first time and a fallback to the phone's own font if the download fails.

Worth it if you want the apps to look like one family. Not worth it if you would rather they stay instant and never wobble.
